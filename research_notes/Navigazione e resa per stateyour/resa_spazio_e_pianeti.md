Legend: **[V]** = verified in this session by reading the source (page, PDF text, or doc). **[R]** = recalled from prior knowledge, not re-verified in this session; treat as likely but check before quoting numbers. Where only a search-engine summary was seen (not the page itself), it is marked **[S]**.

## Galaxies: how SpaceEngine, Gaia Sky, Elite Dangerous, Universe Sandbox and academic work render them (stars, dust, emission, bulge, HII, spiral noise, LOD, avoiding banding and the "cotton-ball" look, tone mapping)

### Takeaway
None of the shipping space renderers found here renders a whole galaxy as a single uniformly-sampled ray-marched volume. They layer it: (1) additive point/billboard stars, (2) additive soft "gas"/bulge glow, (3) small additive red HII knots placed on the arms, (4) a separate, lower-resolution, **purely absorbing (subtractive/multiplicative) dust layer** made of many small elements, with dust lanes on the *inner* edge of arms. Gaia Sky's documented parameters show dust is made of many (~28k) small, thin elements, not a few big puffs. That is the direct cure for both the "solid wall" and the "cotton-ball" look. SpaceEngine adds brightness/contrast/saturation/gamma controls for galaxies specifically (e.g., gamma 0.7) so the view from inside the disk does not look over-bright.

### Cited Findings
**Gaia Sky (open source, best-documented) [V]**
- The procedural galaxy system is "based on billboard particles, i.e., particles that are always oriented towards the camera", organized in channels of type GAS, HII, STAR, DUST, BULGE, POINT; typical configurations use 3–6 channels. Particles are generated on the GPU by compute shaders (OpenGL 4.3+), with a CPU fallback — [Gaia Sky docs: Procedural galaxies](https://gaia.ari.uni-heidelberg.de/gaiasky/docs/master/Procedural-galaxies.html)
- Resolution split: STAR and HII are rendered at full resolution; GAS and DUST at a lower resolution, "approximately 4 times fewer pixels" (i.e., half-res in each axis) — [Gaia Sky docs](https://gaia.ari.uni-heidelberg.de/gaiasky/docs/master/Procedural-galaxies.html)
- Blending: stars, gas, HII and bulge are **additive**; DUST is **subtractive**, "particles darken what lies behind them, producing dark dust lanes against the stellar disk". Selecting DUST forces subtractive blending and disables depth writes; all example channels have depthMask = false — [Gaia Sky docs](https://gaia.ari.uni-heidelberg.de/gaiasky/docs/master/Procedural-galaxies.html)
- Example descriptor (numbers from the docs): STAR 25,666 particles, size 0.3, intensity 2, height scale 0.048; HII 430 particles, size 2.2, intensity 1; GAS 3,390 particles, size 51.78, intensity 0.0108, height 0.02; DUST 28,200 particles, size 5.047, intensity 0.05, height 0.031 with a "linear_inc" height profile, FBM size-noise scale 20.17, max size 25; BULGE 5 particles, size 97.5, intensity 0.5, SPHERE distribution. Spiral: 2 arms, eccentricity 0.23, arm sigma 0.187, base angle 347.4° for stars/HII but 704° for gas/dust (dust/gas wound differently from stars). R_min 0.12 (stars) vs 0.19 (gas/dust) — [Gaia Sky docs](https://gaia.ari.uni-heidelberg.de/gaiasky/docs/master/Procedural-galaxies.html)
- "Max size" clamps particle size per graphics-quality level "limiting cost in close-up views"; with FBM on, size noise becomes a 1–50 spatial-frequency scale — [Gaia Sky docs](https://gaia.ari.uni-heidelberg.de/gaiasky/docs/master/Procedural-galaxies.html)
- Distributions available: SPHERE, DISK, DISK_GAUSS, SPHERE_GAUSS, SPIRAL (density wave, 2 or 4 arms), SPIRAL_LOG (1–8 arms), BAR, ELLIPSOID, CONE, IRREGULAR; warps of −0.1..0.1 auto-applied with morphology-dependent probability (Sa–Sc 60%, SBa–SBc 40%, S0 25%) — [Gaia Sky docs](https://gaia.ari.uni-heidelberg.de/gaiasky/docs/master/Procedural-galaxies.html)
- A later bug-fix release "restores the occlusion of the procedurally generated galaxy channels by the volume dust", i.e., Gaia Sky also has a separate volumetric dust that occludes the billboard channels [S] — [Flathub Gaia Sky](https://flathub.org/kab/apps/space.gaiasky.GaiaSky); 3.7.2 adds billboard shading modes [S] — [Gaia Sky 3.7.2 news](https://gaiasky.space/news/2026/3.7.2-new-features/); overview — [Gaia Sky galaxy generation preview](https://gaiasky.space/news/2025/galaxy-generation/) [V]

**SpaceEngine [V]**
- Galaxy dust bands are built from procedurally distributed **sprites**, tuned to look like the Milky Way's dust band from Earth; more and higher-resolution sprite textures were added to sharpen dust detail when viewed from inside a galaxy; sprites "match the dust texture as the camera approaches a galaxy" (far: texture; near: sprites). Procedural nebulae are **ray-marched**; real nebulae still sprites. Galaxies and nebulae get separate brightness, contrast, saturation and gamma controls; gamma 0.7 vs 1 is shown to stop the Milky Way looking over-bright from inside; LOD switching of sprite models was made more aggressive for performance — [SpaceEngine blog, Feb 2019 "Current work"](https://spaceengine.org/news/blog190217/)
- SpaceEngine's internal galaxy volumetric algorithm is not publicly documented in a paper (I found none) — see Gaps.

**Beltoforion density-wave galaxy renderer (open source, has a WebGL/TypeScript version) [V]**
- Stars are placed on ellipses with the galactic center at a focus; each successive orbit is rotated slightly more, which creates the spiral (density-wave shortcut, no n-body). Eccentricity rises toward the bulge/disk boundary then falls to circular at ~2× disk radius; core kept round. Surface brightness: bulge de Vaucouleurs I(R)=I0·exp(−κR^¼), disk Freeman exponential I(R)=I0·exp(−R/R_D). Star temperatures 3000–9000 K, colors from blackbody (specrend.c). ~60,000 stars. HII regions are drawn as red nebula sprites whose size is proportional to the spacing between adjacent density waves, with a cutoff, so they appear mainly in the arms — [Beltoforion: Rendering a galaxy with the density wave theory](https://beltoforion.de/en/spiral_galaxy_renderer)
- Astrophysical placement rule: the principal dust lanes lie on the inner (concave) edge of the bright optical arms; HII regions make arms look red/pink [S] — [Wikipedia: Density wave theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Density_wave_theory); [Arizona lecture notes on spiral arms](https://ircamera.as.arizona.edu/NatSci102/NatSci/lectures/spiralarms.htm)

**Volumetric integration (applies to any ray-marched galaxy/nebula)**
- [R] Hillaire (SIGGRAPH 2015, Frostbite) "energy-conserving" analytic integration per step: with scattered radiance S and extinction σt constant over a step of length d, integrate S·∫exp(−σt x)dx = (S − S·exp(−σt d))/σt, instead of S·d; removes brightness dependence on step size and the over-bright/"wall" artefacts in dense media. Talk confirmed to exist (froxels, extinction volume, validated against Mitsuba) [S] — [Frostbite: Physically based unified volumetric rendering](https://www.ea.com/frostbite/news/physically-based-unified-volumetric-rendering-in-frostbite); [slides](https://www.slideshare.net/DICEStudio/physically-based-and-unified-volumetric-rendering-in-frostbite)
- [R] Wrenninge et al. ("Oz: The Great and Volumetric", 2013) multiple-scattering approximation, reused by Hillaire 2016 for clouds: sum N octaves of single scattering with σs·bⁱ, extinction·aⁱ, phase anisotropy g·cⁱ (a,b,c ≤ 1). Cheap way to give dust/gas a soft luminous interior without full multiple scattering — [Hillaire 2016 Frostbite sky/clouds course notes](https://media.contentapi.ea.com/content/dam/eacom/frostbite/files/s2016_pbs_frostbite_sky_clouds.pdf) (PDF exists [S]; octave formula [R])

### Inferences
- **The "solid wall" of dust** is consistent with (a) using too high an extinction relative to the step length without analytic per-step integration, and (b) a dust layer as thick as the stellar disk. Gaia Sky's dust is thinner (height 0.031 vs stars 0.048 in the same units) and made of many small elements; real dust scale height is much smaller than the stellar thick disk. Make the dust density a thin exponential in z (sech² or exp(−|z|/h_dust) with h_dust ≈ 0.3–0.5 × h_stars) and clamp optical depth per step.
- **The "cotton-ball" look** typically comes from isotropic, low-frequency, round fBm blobs with soft edges that are lit/emissive. All the references avoid it in two ways: (1) dust is *absorption only* (subtractive/multiplicative), so it reads as dark filaments silhouetted against starlight rather than as lit puffs; (2) structure is many small, stretched elements aligned with the arm (density-wave orbits or log-spiral coordinates). Practical recipe: evaluate noise in (log-spiral angle, radius, z) coordinates so features are stretched along arms; use ridged/turbulent noise (1−|n|) and domain warping for filaments; modulate by arm phase so dust peaks on the inner edge of the arm and HII/young blue stars just downstream.
- **Edge-on artefacts**: a uniform step through a thin disk under-samples it edge-on. Options: march in disk-local space with step length proportional to the local scale height (smaller near z=0), or integrate the vertical exponential analytically per segment; jitter the ray start with blue noise; use optical-depth-based early-out.
- **Far-to-near LOD**: SpaceEngine (texture far → sprites near) and Gaia Sky (max-size clamp per quality level; half-res dust/gas) suggest: far = precomputed 2D/low-res 3D texture of emission & optical depth; medium = ray-march a coarse 3D density; inside disk = add near-field dust sprites or high-frequency noise octaves that fade in with footprint (pixel cone width) so detail is band-limited and doesn't shimmer.
- Separate artistic controls (brightness, contrast, saturation, gamma per object class, like SpaceEngine) are a pragmatic layer on top of physical exposure.

## Nebulae and interstellar medium volumetrics in real time

### Takeaway
Real-time nebulae in Gaia Sky and SpaceEngine are full-screen post-process ray-marchers inside a bounding sphere, depth-tested against the scene, adapted from Shadertoy shaders; the production-quality optimizations come from cloud rendering (Nubis): two-level noise (cheap shape, expensive erosion detail only where shape is non-zero), adaptive step size, early exit, quarter-res rendering with 1/16 pixel updates per frame plus reprojection.

### Cited Findings
- Gaia Sky nebulae (Cat's Eye, Hourglass, Trifid, Butterfly, Crab) are ray-marched in a post-processing full-screen pass that receives the frame buffer, the depth buffer and noise textures; volume bounded by a sphere (ray–sphere near/far), loop accumulating density and color; mostly regular alpha blending, additive possible; shaders adapted from Shadertoy author "Duke" (e.g., "Dusty Nebula 4", "Supernova Remnant"); example Crab shader at shadertoy.com/view/MfcBzH. Fix for mirrored ghosting when inside the volume: only sample points on the correct side of the ray — [Toni Sagristà, Rendering volume aurorae and nebulae (2024)](https://tonisagrista.com/blog/2024/rendering-aurorae-nebulae/) [V]
- SpaceEngine ray-marches procedural nebulae; real catalogued nebulae remain sprites — [SpaceEngine blog 2019](https://spaceengine.org/news/blog190217/) [V]
- Horizon Zero Dawn clouds (Schneider & Vos, SIGGRAPH 2015) [V, from PDF text]: base-shape 3D texture 128³, 4 channels (channel 1 Perlin-Worley, channels 2–4 Worley at increasing frequencies); detail 3D texture 32³, 3 channels of Worley at increasing frequencies; 2D 128², 3-channel curl noise ("non divergent ... used to fake fluid motion") to distort shapes and add turbulence; high-detail noise "always applied as an erosion from the edge of the base cloud shape"; cheap large steps until hitting the iso-surface, then step back one step and switch to full samples; stop when alpha reaches 1; after several consecutive zero-density samples go back to cheap marching; "an initial potential 64 samples" up to "potential 128 at the horizon"; 6 light samples in a cone toward the sun, last one far away for distant shadows; switching light samples to cheap version once alpha reached 0.3 made it 2× faster; lighting = Beer's law × Henyey-Greenstein × "powdered sugar" term (dark edges facing the light, view-dependent) + extra absorption for rain clouds; colour = ambient increasing with height + sun-coloured direct, attenuated toward atmosphere colour by depth; first version cost ~20 ms; final: quarter-res buffer updating 1 of 16 pixels per 4×4 block per frame with reprojection of the previous frame, low-res fallback at screen edges, "10x faster or more", target ~2 ms — [Schneider & Vos, The Real-time Volumetric Cloudscapes of Horizon Zero Dawn (Advances 2015)](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2015/The%20Real-time%20Volumetric%20Cloudscapes%20of%20Horizon%20-%20Zero%20Dawn%20-%20ARTR.pdf)
- [R] The powder term is commonly implemented as `2·exp(−d)·(1−exp(−2d))` (Beer–Powder); the slides name it but the formula wasn't in the extracted text.
- Later Nubis talks: 2017 "Nubis: Authoring Real-Time Volumetric Cloudscapes with the Decima Engine" (within a 2 ms budget), 2022 "Nubis, Evolved", 2023 "Nubis Cubed" (voxel/SDF-based cloud volumes [R]) — [Advances 2017 PDF](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2017/Nubis%20-%20Authoring%20Realtime%20Volumetric%20Cloudscapes%20with%20the%20Decima%20Engine%20-%20Final%20.pdf); [Advances 2022 PDF](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2022/SIGGRAPH2022-Advances-NubisEvolved-NoVideos.pdf); [Advances 2023 PDF](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2023/Nubis%20Cubed%20(Advances%202023).pdf) [S]
- Other references: Magnor et al. reflection-nebula GPU radiative transfer — [vis05.pdf](https://www.graphics.tu-bs.de/upload/people/magnor/publications/vis05.pdf); "Rendering Nebulae" blog — [pegwars](https://pegwars.blogspot.com/2018/12/rendering-nebulae.html) [S]

### Inferences
- For stateyour's galaxy volume, the Nubis pattern maps directly: coarse "envelope" density (arm/disk/bulge analytic profile) → only where envelope > 0, evaluate high-frequency erosion noise. That also kills the cotton-ball look, because erosion carves filaments out of the edges instead of adding round puffs.
- Emission nebulae: emission should be an independent channel (Hα red 656 nm, OIII teal) with dust absorption on top; reflection nebulae: scattering of nearby starlight with forward-peaked HG phase (g≈0.6, [R]).

## Stars: PSF, diffraction spikes, magnitude-to-brightness mapping, bloom, crisp starfields at all scales

### Takeaway
State of the art (Schneegans et al., Eurographics 2025, open source in CosmoScout VR) computes each star's luminance from the solid angle of its proxy, so sub-pixel stars conserve energy regardless of projection/pixel solid angle, then applies a perception-based glare filter (computed in perspective-correct space) to spread energy of bright stars. Gaia Sky maps magnitude → luminosity → pseudo-size via sqrt, computes solid angle, and applies a brightness power function to stretch contrast.

### Cited Findings
- "Smaller than Pixels: Rendering Millions of Stars in Real-Time" (Schneegans, Kreskowski, Gerndt, EG 2025 short): most stars are sub-pixel; luminance must be spread over a pixel or proxy geometry; edge pixels subtend a smaller solid angle so single-pixel stars there need proportionally higher brightness (matters for wide FOV/VR/domes); naive points make centre stars too bright → include projected size in luminance; perception-based glare filter spreads luminance to neighbours, computed in perspective-correct space (ellipsoidal glare) rather than screen space; their software rasterizer beats classic techniques almost always; 50 M Gaia stars (~22 M in frustum) at ~8 ms, 4K, RTX 4070 Super; code in github.com/cosmoscout/cosmoscout-vr — [Uni Weimar project page](https://www.uni-weimar.de/de/medien/professuren/medieninformatik/vr/research/real-time-rendering/smaller-than-pixels-rendering-millions-of-stars-in-real-tim/) [V]; [paper PDF, EG diglib](https://diglib.eg.org/bitstreams/7762e358-2fa8-4542-8d30-05a88798373c/download) [S]
- Gaia Sky: apparent magnitude (extinction-corrected) → absolute M = m − 5(log10 d_pc − 1) → L = L0·10^(−0.4·M) → pseudo-size = const·sqrt(L); solid angle α = atan(p/d) or small-angle p/d; brightness power function on solid angle to widen bright/faint contrast; billboards (perspective-correct) or GL_POINTS (faster, screen-space); shader star.group.quad.vertex.glsl — [Gaia Sky docs: Star rendering](https://gaia.ari.uni-heidelberg.de/gaiasky/docs/3.6.4/Star-rendering.html) [V]
- Outerra has a stars rendering screenshot set (no technique text found) — [moddb](https://www.moddb.com/engines/outerra/images/stars-rendering1) [S]
- [R] Photometric anchor: flux ratio = 10^(−0.4·Δm); m = 0 corresponds to an illuminance of ≈2.5×10⁻⁶ lux at the observer; the Sun (m ≈ −26.74) ≈ 1.2–1.3×10⁵ lux. With a physical eye-adaptation pipeline this gives an absolute star illuminance to deposit into a pixel (divide by pixel solid angle → luminance).
- [R] Physically based glare: Spencer, Shirley, Zimmerman, Greenberg 1995 "Physically-based glare effects for digital images"; Ritschel et al. 2009 "Temporal glare: real-time dynamic simulation of the scattering in the human eye" (FFT of an aperture/eyelash/pupil mask gives the PSF and spikes). Diffraction spikes = Fourier transform of the aperture; a 4-vane spider gives 4 spikes (telescope look); a hexagonal aperture 6 spikes (JWST-like 6+2).

### Inferences
- Keep stars crisp: render them in linear HDR as energy-conserving points (area-weighted sub-pixel footprint, e.g., a 2×2 Gaussian/bilinear splat whose integral = star flux/pixel solid angle), never as fixed-size sprites; let bloom/glare (convolution with a PSF whose wings scale with pixel brightness) make bright stars look bigger. Apply bloom *after* exposure but *before* tone mapping, at low strength (wide PSF with ~1–4% energy in the wings [R]) so faint stars stay pin-sharp.
- Temporal stability: a star crossing pixel boundaries flickers if rendered as point-sampled single pixels; energy-conserving splats remove this.
- Use temperature → blackbody chromaticity, but desaturate faint stars (scotopic vision has no colour, [R] Purkinje/mesopic models) for a legible look consistent with physical eye adaptation.

## Planets: atmospheres, clouds, planetary-scale procedural terrain, texturing and detail synthesis

### Takeaway
stateyour already uses Hillaire 2020; Bruneton's 2017 code has an official WebGL2 demo, and the takram "three-geospatial" packages ship a Bruneton atmosphere plus volumetric clouds (shadow maps, temporal upscaling, light shafts) for three.js, with a WebGPU port in progress. For terrain, the shipping planet engines (Outerra, NMS) combine real/coarse data or voxel fields with fractal refinement and per-axis filtered slopes, plus logarithmic depth for planetary ranges.

### Cited Findings
**Atmospheres**
- Bruneton "Precomputed Atmospheric Scattering" 2017 revision: WebGL2 demo online with source in the `webgl` subdirectory; converts spectral radiance to RGB luminance (or precomputes luminance directly) — "almost the same results" as full spectral at a fraction of the cost; configurable solar spectrum; ozone layer on by default; custom density profiles for molecules and aerosols; partial light-shaft support — [Bruneton, precomputed_atmospheric_scattering](https://ebruneton.github.io/precomputed_atmospheric_scattering/) [V]
- Hillaire 2020 "A Scalable and Production Ready Sky and Atmosphere Rendering Technique" (EGSR/CGF): set of small LUTs and parameterizations for sky and aerial perspective, used in Unreal; scales mobile→high-end by LUT resolution and sample count — [EG diglib](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/cgf14050); [SIGGRAPH 2020 slides](https://blog.selfshadow.com/publications/s2020-shading-course/hillaire/s2020_pbs_hillaire_slides.pdf) [S]. A community DX11 implementation uses a 64×64 sky-view and 64×64×32 aerial-perspective LUT; sky-view generation 0.036 ms on GTX 1060 — [AirGuanZ/AtmosphereRenderer](https://www.Github.com/AirGuanZ/AtmosphereRenderer) [S]. [R] Paper defaults: transmittance 256×64, multiple-scattering 32×32, sky-view 192×108, aerial perspective 32×32×32.
- three.js: `@takram/three-atmosphere` = Bruneton precomputed scattering for three.js/R3F with post-process, light-source and mixed lighting; `@takram/three-clouds` = volumetric clouds with Beer shadow maps, temporal upscaling and filtering, light shafts, to be placed before the aerial-perspective pass; needs pmndrs `postprocessing` EffectComposer; viewpoint must stay above the inner sphere; WebGPU atmosphere done, clouds in progress — [npm @takram/three-clouds](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@takram/three-clouds); [three-atmosphere README](https://unpkg.com/@takram/three-atmosphere@0.11.1/README.md); [three-geospatial README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@takram/three-geospatial@0.8.0/README.md) [S]

**Clouds**
- Horizon Zero Dawn / Nubis details above (64–128 steps, 6 cone light samples, quarter-res 1/16 update + reprojection, ~2 ms; volumetric strato clouds 1500–4000 m, 2D scrolling alto/cirro textures above; ray marching through a spherical shell makes clouds descend to the horizon and "we can force the scale of the scene by shrinking the radius of the atmosphere") — [Advances 2015 PDF](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2015/The%20Real-time%20Volumetric%20Cloudscapes%20of%20Horizon%20-%20Zero%20Dawn%20-%20ARTR.pdf) [V]

- Optimisations for real-time volumetric cloudscapes (arXiv 1609.05344): random per-pixel ray-start offset recovers structure but is noisy; Playdead TAA on a low-res buffer cleans it; half-res 8-step jittered render 2.3→7.5 ms attributed to cache misses [S] — [arXiv 1609.05344](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.05344)

Claims about domain warping in NMS come only from secondary blogs — treat as unverified.
- Elite Dangerous planets: [PC Gamer: the science behind Elite's planets](https://www.pcgamer.com/the-mind-bending-science-behind-the-planets-of-elite-dangerous) [S, not read]
- [R] Proland (Bruneton & Neyret, INRIA): quadtree of terrain tiles on a cube-sphere, GPU "producers" generating elevation/normal/ortho tiles on demand with residual upsampling (fractal amplification of coarse DEM), plus Bruneton's ocean (seamless geometry→BRDF transition) and forest rendering. [R] Sebastian Lague's "Coding Adventure" videos (Procedural Planets, Atmosphere, Clouds, Solar System) are accessible tutorials using cube-sphere meshes, ridge noise for mountains, and a simple single-scattering atmosphere. [R] Inigo Quilez: "fbm with derivatives" (erosion-like look by damping octaves where slope is high) and domain warping articles at iquilezles.org.

### Inferences
- For "blocky coastlines" (task list mention): coastlines need sub-texel distance or signed height interpolation (bicubic or derivative-aware) + a high-frequency noise octave that only perturbs height near sea level; Outerra's per-axis filtered slope approach and IQ's derivative-damped fBm are the relevant tools.
- Texturing by slope/altitude/biome: blend weights from (altitude, slope = 1 − n·up, latitude/temperature, moisture), with height-based blending between materials and triplanar mapping on steep slopes [R], is the common approach.

## Tone mapping and color grading in space renderers; physical exposure vs artistic legibility

### Takeaway
three.js ships ACES Filmic, AgX (r160+) and Khronos PBR Neutral (r162+). AgX keeps highlight detail and desaturates very bright colours gracefully (good for bright galaxy cores and stars), ACES is more saturated/contrasty with hue shifts, Khronos Neutral preserves base colours 1:1 up to a threshold. Space renderers add per-class gamma/contrast/saturation controls on top of physical exposure (SpaceEngine).

### Cited Findings
- three.js/pmndrs postprocessing: AGX filmic requires three r160+, NEUTRAL (Khronos) r162+ — [pmndrs ToneMappingMode](https://pmndrs.github.io/postprocessing/public/docs/file/src/enums/ToneMappingMode.js.html); discussion of correctness of three.js AgX — [three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/is-agx-tonemapping-implemented-correctly/60609); overview — [three.js tone mapping overview](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/tone-mapping-overview/75204) [S]

### Inferences
- With physical eye adaptation, a galaxy seen from outside spans a huge range (core vs. outer disk) and goes to the shoulder; AgX-style "path to white" makes the core white instead of hue-shifted orange. ## WebGL/WebGPU examples, libraries and performance tricks (temporal reprojection, half-res volumetrics, blue-noise jitter)

### Takeaway
The proven budget tricks are: render volumetrics at half/quarter resolution; jitter the ray start with blue noise (ideally spatiotemporal blue noise) to turn banding into fine noise; accumulate temporally with reprojection (HZD: update 1/16 pixels per frame); depth/luminance-aware upsampling/blur; two-level density (cheap envelope, expensive detail); early exit at alpha ≈ 1; LOD of noise octaves with distance.

### Cited Findings
- three.js "Efficient volumetric clouds": tileable 128³ Perlin-Worley 3D texture (~2 MB) sampled at multiple frequencies; logarithmic z spacing of primary samples (~10× fewer z samples than XY coverage); light rays linear, ≤ ~1000 m, far samples faded; fewer light samples for distant/low-density samples; FBM octave LOD with blended transitions; blue-noise jitter of sample positions (Calinou/free-blue-noise-textures); edge-preserving Kawase blur with blue-noise-jittered taps weighted by luminance/depth similarity; temporal accumulation tried and abandoned for fuzzy edges; WebGL2 repo github.com/Spiri0/volumetric-clouds generating Worley noise on GPU with compute + storage 3D textures (WebGPU) — [three.js forum thread](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/efficient-volumetric-clouds/66067) [V]
- Spatiotemporal blue noise (EA SEED, EGSR 2022): reusing one blue-noise mask per frame gives white-noise behaviour over time and unstable TAA; STBN masks are blue in space and time, demonstrated on volumetrics — [EA SEED: Scalar Spatiotemporal Blue Noise Masks](https://www.ea.com/seed/news/egsr-2022-blue-noise) [S]
- HZD temporal scheme: quarter-res, 1 of 16 pixels per 4×4 block updated per frame, reprojection, low-res fallback where reprojection fails; ≥10× speed-up; ~2 ms target — [Advances 2015 PDF](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2015/The%20Real-time%20Volumetric%20Cloudscapes%20of%20Horizon%20-%20Zero%20Dawn%20-%20ARTR.pdf) [V]
- Half-res dust/gas channels in Gaia Sky (~4× fewer pixels) — [Gaia Sky docs](https://gaia.ari.uni-heidelberg.de/gaiasky/docs/master/Procedural-galaxies.html) [V]
- Other libraries: Bruneton WebGL2 atmosphere demo — [ebruneton.github.io](https://ebruneton.github.io/precomputed_atmospheric_scattering/) [V]; takram three-geospatial (atmosphere + clouds, WebGPU port) [S] (links above); Beltoforion galaxy renderer TypeScript/WebGL version — [beltoforion](https://beltoforion.de/en/spiral_galaxy_renderer) [V]; volumetric optimizations write-up — [emmyvoita](https://emmyvoita.github.io/posts/Real-Time-Optimizations-For-Volumetric-Rendering) [S]; Godot shaders "Volumetric cosmic dust" / "Volumetric nebulae" for reference code — [godotshaders](https://godotshaders.com/shader/volumetric-cosmic-dust/) [S]

### Inferences
- For stateyour (Electron = Chromium, so WebGL2 now and WebGPU available): put the galaxy volume in a half-res (or quarter-res + 1/16 temporal) HDR target (RGBA16F: rgb emission-in-scatter, a = transmittance), jitter ray start with STBN indexed by frame, reproject using the previous view-projection (camera-only motion is easy since galaxy is static), clamp history in a neighbourhood colour box, then bilateral-upsample with depth. Stars stay at full res as energy-conserving points composited *behind* the volume transmittance (multiply star radiance by volume transmittance along their ray) — this is what makes dust lanes silhouette crisply against stars.
- WebGPU compute is a good fit for generating 3D noise textures and for a Schneegans-style software star rasterizer (atomics into a storage buffer).

### Gaps
- No complete open WebGL/WebGPU galaxy renderer of SpaceEngine quality was found; the best browser-side building blocks are separate (atmosphere, clouds, star point rasterization, density-wave galaxy).
- No measured timings for WebGL2 volumetric galaxies found.
